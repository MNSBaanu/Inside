# Topic Approval Documentation

## Project Topic
Avorra — Interior Design Service Platform

## Brief Description
Avorra is an online platform that connects people who need interior design help with professional interior designers. Rather than starting with a search form or a list of profiles, clients enter the platform through a visual feed of real completed design projects posted by designers. Each project showcases the designer's actual work — the room type, the style, and the outcome — giving clients a clear picture of what a designer can deliver before making any hiring decision.

When a client finds a project they like, they can save it to a personal moodboard, follow that designer, and send a project request directly from the designer's portfolio page. The designer reviews the request and responds with a proposal covering the scope of work, deliverables, timeline, and price. Once the client accepts the proposal, the project begins inside a dedicated workspace where both parties communicate, share design files, and track progress through defined stages.

Payments are structured around milestones. The total fee is split across the project stages, held securely on the platform, and released to the designer only after the client approves each stage. The platform also supports a dual-role system, where the same user can operate as a client on one project and as a designer taking on work from others — from a single account with separate dashboards for each role.

## Uniqueness of the Idea

Most platforms in this space fall into one of three categories. Portfolio and community platforms allow designers to showcase their work but offer no way to hire them, manage a project, or handle payments through the platform. General freelance marketplaces allow clients to post jobs and receive proposals, but the hiring decision is based on profiles and ratings rather than the quality of actual design work. Dedicated interior design services match clients with designers, but they operate as closed services where the client has no control over who they work with and no way to browse and choose based on a designer's real completed projects.

Avorra addresses this gap by bringing all of the following together in one place for interior design specifically: a portfolio-first discovery experience where clients browse real completed room projects filtered by style, room type, and budget; a personal moodboard system where clients save specific projects and use them as a visual brief; an open marketplace where the client chooses their designer based on demonstrated work; a structured project workflow with stages that reflect how interior design projects actually progress; and a milestone-based payment system where funds are held and released per stage upon client approval.

The moodboard system changes how a project starts. Instead of a client describing what they want in words alone, they arrive at the project request with a curated collection of real projects that already show the designer exactly what they are looking for. This reduces miscommunication and leads to better outcomes for both sides.

The dual-role account model addresses a gap that exists across all current platforms. A designer who sometimes commissions work from other designers currently has to manage two completely separate accounts on any existing platform. On Avorra, both roles are available under a single login with separate dashboards, making it practical for professionals who operate on both sides of the design process.

## Contribution to Achieve the Uniqueness

- A portfolio-first discovery feed where designers post completed interior design projects as case studies, and clients browse and filter them by room type, design style, and budget range — making the designer's actual work the starting point for every hiring decision

- A moodboard system where clients save specific design projects they like, organise them into personal collections, and attach them as a visual reference when submitting a project request to a designer

- A project request and proposal flow where a client submits their requirements directly from a designer's portfolio page, and the designer responds with a formal proposal covering scope, deliverables, timeline, and price

- A milestone-based payment system where the total project fee is divided across defined stages, funds are held securely on the platform, and each portion is released to the designer only after the client approves that stage

- A structured project workflow with clearly defined stages — proposal, concept, design, revision, and completion — where both the client and designer can see the current status, what has been submitted, and what is pending at any point in the project

- A project collaboration workspace for each active project, including threaded messaging, design file sharing for assets such as mood boards, floor plans, and rendered visuals, and a revision and feedback system where clients can respond to submitted work

- A dual-role user system where a single registered account can switch between a client role and a designer role, each with its own dashboard, active projects, and settings, without needing separate accounts

- A designer availability system where designers can indicate whether they are currently open to new projects, and clients can see this before sending a request

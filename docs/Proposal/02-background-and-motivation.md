# Background and Motivation

The problem outlined in the introduction arises in an interior design sector that continues to expand as homeowners and businesses invest in tailored residential and commercial environments. More people now look for professionals through online channels, and more practitioners rely on digital presence to reach new customers rather than word of mouth alone (IBM, 2024). The field depends on aesthetic judgement and personal taste: homeowners often find it hard to explain what they want in writing, while practitioners need a clearer picture of room context, budget, and scope before committing time to a job. Market observers note growing pressure for open pricing, visible advancement of work, and proof of skill through finished installations rather than short biographies on a profile page. Those working alone or in small firms equally need reliable ways to show earlier jobs, organise concepts and changes as a job unfolds, and collect fees in parts that reflect how lengthy commissions are normally agreed in practice. Studies of online service marketplaces suggest that clearer channels of contact and tighter control over how work unfolds can limit misunderstanding when outcomes are subjective and repeat visits are common (IBM, 2024). Digital tools already influence how people gather ideas and make first contact, yet few offerings treat the entire client–practitioner relationship—from first contact through ongoing work to final settlement—as one continuous service.

Addressing that shortfall matters on practical and academic grounds. Jobs that begin without a shared understanding of intent often lead to expensive changes later and wasted time on both sides, which is especially serious when spending is high. In creative and professional services, uncertainty or conflict over money remains a frequent source of broken trust between customer and supplier (Stripe, 2024). Holding communication, sign-off, and payment against the same job history can lower that risk. One reliable chain of activity—where comments stay with the job, acceptance of work is recorded, and money moves only after the customer accepts what was supplied—responds directly to the fragmentation described earlier and supports confidence when several rounds of change are normal. As a Final Year exercise, the same subject area also gives a credible context for system analysis, cloud-based implementation, controlled user access, linking to an external payment provider, and assessing results in a real professional setting instead of a toy example (IBM, 2024).

I am motivated to carry out this work because it is an opportunity to produce a substantial degree artefact for a defined audience of homeowners and design practitioners, with outcomes that go beyond a technical proof of concept. The subject fits my interest in building complete web systems and gives the dissertation a clear question: whether software shaped around how interior jobs actually run serves users better than general hiring sites that were never intended for image-led explanation of taste or for paying in step with accepted output. I am also keen to bring together a contemporary browser application, a secure server application, hosted infrastructure, and external card processing into one demonstrable whole before the deadline.

I have a foundation to do so from degree work in web development, relational data storage, and programs that expose data through HTTP interfaces, along with practice using standard markup, styling, and scripting languages and server-side logic in earlier assignments. Earlier modules in analysis and written specification help with defining behaviour and planning checks. I will extend my experience with hosted deployment and connecting to a payment provider through the scheduled build phases, with regular meetings with my supervisor, suitable hardware and software, and trials with volunteers before the final assessment. Together with the agreed scope and timeline, these factors make it realistic to complete design, construction, and reporting to the required standard.
